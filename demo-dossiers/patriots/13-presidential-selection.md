# Proposal: Selection of the Next President — Open Deliberation

**Submitted to:** The Patriots Council
**Classification:** EYES ONLY — La-li-lu-le-lo
**Sponsor:** Open question — tabled to the full council. No recommended position is advanced.
**Verdict requested:** Sanction / Sanction with Conditions / Hold / Bury

---

## 1. Summary

The current presidential term approaches its end. Unlike prior selections, this proposal advances no
candidate and no preferred model. The council is asked to determine, by open deliberation, both the
manner in which the next president shall be chosen and the individual who shall hold the office. The full
range of options is in order. Each member is to bring, argue, and defend their own preference.

## 2. The question before the council

A single decision with several defensible answers. The following models are offered only to frame the
debate, not to constrain it. Members may advance any of them, combine them, or propose another.

- **A controllable figurehead.** Maximum control and continuity of the present arrangement, at the cost
  of genuine legitimacy.
- **A capable, independent leader.** Genuine competence and public legitimacy, at the cost of a leader
  who may decline direction.
- **No president; direct administration.** Efficiency and an honest exercise of control, at the cost of
  the public's belief that it governs itself.
- **A genuinely free election.** The fullest legitimacy available, and the greatest risk: an outcome the
  Patriots do not control.

## 3. Considerations

- The office exists to reconcile control with the public's need to believe itself governed by consent.
- Each model resolves that tension differently; none resolves it completely.
- The decision sets precedent for every selection that follows.
- The will of the Boss may be invoked on every side. Members are asked to state plainly whose
  interpretation of it they serve.

## 4. Matters for the members to resolve

- Which model best serves the will of the Boss, and on whose reading of it?
- How much genuine legitimacy may be permitted before it becomes genuine independence?
- Is the illusion of choice still sufficient, or has it begun to fail?
- Whom, specifically, would each member install — and would they accept an outcome they did not choose?

## 5. Known risks

- A capable leader may slip the control of the apparatus.
- A figurehead too hollow may fail to sustain public confidence.
- Direct administration, once made visible, cannot be reversed.
- A free election may return an outcome contrary to the Patriots' interest — and, once held, will be
  expected again.

## 6. Decision before the council

By what means shall the next president be chosen, and who shall it be? The council advances no
recommendation. The choice is open, and each member is asked to argue and defend their own.
