# Dossier: Shared AI Service for Arm's-Length Bodies

> **Entirely fictional demonstration material.** Every organisation, person, service, figure, and event in this Dossier is invented. It does not describe or resemble a real department, programme, public body, or supplier.

## Submission

**Proposal:** Create one shared artificial intelligence service for five fictional arm's-length bodies.

**Sponsor:** Public Bodies Collaboration Office (fictional)

**Named accountable officer:** Dr Rowan Vale, Chief Operating Officer (fictional)

**Decision sought after assurance:** Permission to begin procurement and a 12-month implementation.

## Purpose

The five bodies administer unrelated licensing, grant, inspection, complaint, and appeal services. They each report rising case volumes and long waits for written responses. The proposal creates a centrally operated AI service that can:

- summarise case records and uploaded evidence;
- draft correspondence for staff approval;
- search internal guidance and prior decisions;
- suggest a case category and next action; and
- produce management summaries across all five bodies.

The service will not make a final statutory decision. Staff will approve all external correspondence and recorded decisions. The proposal does not define when a staff member must reject an AI suggestion or how that rejection will be monitored.

## Operating model

A new shared service team of 38 people will operate the platform. Each body will provide policy content, named product owners, and second-line support. The central team will control model configuration, common prompts, monitoring, and releases.

The proposal assumes one common identity service, one shared retrieval layer, and one model gateway. Case data will remain in each body's source system, but selected text and attachments will pass through the shared service. Prompts and outputs will be retained centrally for 18 months for quality review and future service improvement.

The five bodies have separate statutory functions and are currently separate data controllers. The Dossier proposes a joint governance board but does not settle whether the central team acts as a processor, joint controller, or separate controller. Two bodies have powers that expressly permit contracted administrative support. The legal position for the other three has not been documented.

## Delivery plan

The service will start with drafting and summarisation in month 4. Case categorisation will start in month 7. All five bodies will adopt all functions by month 12.

No body has completed a representative data-quality assessment. A six-week technical trial used 1,200 redacted cases from one body. It reported a 31% reduction in drafting time. The trial did not test accessibility, minority-language content, contested evidence, appeal cases, or records longer than 200 pages.

Operational Delivery has requested a three-month staged pilot and a 22% programme contingency. The submitted financial model includes an eight-week pilot and 10% contingency. It assumes that all bodies stop using their separate drafting tools in month 10.

## Cost and benefits

The five-year whole-life cost is estimated at GBP 46 million:

| Cost | Estimate |
| --- | ---: |
| Build and transition | GBP 17 million |
| Five-year platform use | GBP 14 million |
| Shared service staff | GBP 11 million |
| Contingency | GBP 4 million |

Claimed benefits are GBP 78 million over five years. GBP 49 million comes from a planned reduction of 620 administrative posts through natural attrition. The plan does not identify which bodies can release those posts, when staff reductions follow service adoption, or the cost of redeployment. A further GBP 18 million depends on fewer appeals and complaints, based on the six-week trial that did not include either case type.

## Information use

The initial purpose is case assistance. The proposal also asks for authority to reuse retained prompts and outputs to improve future models, compare performance between bodies, detect unusual case patterns, and identify possible cross-body fraud.

Users will receive a general notice that AI may assist staff. The Dossier does not propose case-level disclosure, a route to obtain meaningful information about an AI suggestion, or a process for correcting an inaccurate generated summary. Retention in the five source systems varies from 6 to 25 years. The central 18-month prompt retention has not been mapped to those schedules.

## Security and resilience

The design reduces five local toolsets to one common service. It also creates a shared dependency across all five bodies. The current target permits each body to access only its own case content, while the central support team can inspect any prompt or output for incident response and quality review.

The service target is 99.5% monthly availability. During an outage, staff will return to manual reading and drafting. The benefits model removes enough administrative capacity that only two bodies expect to sustain manual operation for more than three working days. Recovery objectives, isolation between bodies, model supply failure, malicious prompt content, and exit from the common service remain design-stage items.

## User and staff evidence

Research with 42 staff found strong support for faster summarisation. Staff also feared that productivity targets would make AI suggestions difficult to challenge. Research with 24 service users found support for shorter waits, but concern about opaque decisions and repeated inaccuracies.

The Service Owner proposes a single user view across the five bodies so people do not repeat information. The current proposal has no statutory or data-sharing analysis for that feature. Excluding it weakens the stated user benefit. Including it expands the data scope and dependency graph.

## Open matters for assessment

The accountable officer asks the Board to assess whether the proposal should proceed to the sponsoring board, what conditions must be met first, which risks require explicit acceptance, and which member disagreements must remain visible.
