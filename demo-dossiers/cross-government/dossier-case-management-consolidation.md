# Dossier: Consolidation of Two Case Management Systems

> **Entirely fictional demonstration material.** Every organisation, person, system, figure, and event in this Dossier is invented. It does not describe or resemble a real department, programme, public body, or supplier.

## Submission

**Proposal:** Consolidate two case management systems into one shared platform.

**Sponsors:** Civic Support Agency and Independent Decisions Office (both fictional)

**Named accountable officer:** Morgan Ellery, Director General for Shared Operations (fictional)

**Decision sought after assurance:** Permission to approve the full business case and start migration.

## Current services

The Civic Support Agency manages applications for four forms of discretionary support. Its fictional internal system, **Northbank**, contains 3.8 million current and closed cases. The Independent Decisions Office reviews disputed eligibility and enforcement decisions. Its fictional internal system, **Clearway**, contains 920,000 cases.

People often move from an application in Northbank to a review in Clearway. They must resubmit evidence because the bodies have separate records, teams, and statutory functions. Staff exchange selected documents by secure transfer. Average transfer time is nine working days.

Northbank is expensive to change but stable. Clearway has better workflow and accessibility features, but its infrastructure support ends in 26 months. Both systems contain health, financial, family, identity, and alleged misconduct information.

## Proposed platform

The proposal will replace both systems with **CommonCase**, a new fictional internal platform. CommonCase will provide one person record, one document store, shared identity matching, configurable workflows, and a combined staff search.

The stated purpose is to reduce repeat evidence, improve hand-offs, and retire both existing systems. Staff in each body will normally see only their own workflow. A cross-body support team will have broad search access. Review staff will be able to open the full originating case when they consider it relevant, rather than request selected evidence.

The legal teams agree that each body can operate a case management system. They have not agreed that either body's existing powers support a common person record, routine access to the full originating case, or management of the other body's records. The proposal assumes that a data-sharing agreement can resolve these points.

## Migration and delivery

Delivery will take 20 months, leaving six months before Clearway support ends. The programme will configure CommonCase, cleanse and migrate 4.72 million cases, train 4,600 staff, and move 68 interfaces.

The migration plan copies all open cases and 12 years of closed cases. Sampling found duplicate identity records in 14% of Northbank cases and incomplete retention markers in 27% of Clearway cases. The schedule allows eight weeks for data reconciliation.

Operational Delivery recommends two regional pilots, six months of parallel running, and a tested manual fallback. This adds GBP 13 million and seven months. Finance recommends one regional pilot, eight weeks of parallel running, and retirement of Northbank immediately after national cutover to protect the savings case.

## Cost and benefits

The seven-year whole-life cost is estimated at GBP 112 million. The estimate includes 12% contingency. The independent schedule review gives the plan a 35% confidence of meeting its current date and recommends 28% cost contingency.

The business case claims GBP 151 million in benefits:

| Benefit | Estimate |
| --- | ---: |
| Retire Northbank and Clearway | GBP 58 million |
| Reduce document handling and transfer | GBP 37 million |
| Reduce staff effort through one person record | GBP 31 million |
| Fewer avoidable reviews and complaints | GBP 25 million |

The retirement benefit requires both legacy systems to close by month 22. The one-person-record benefit assumes that the proposed broad matching and access are lawful. The complaints benefit comes from a small prototype with 36 staff and no service users. Costs for redress after a migration or matching error are excluded.

## Information rights and records

The two bodies are separate data controllers. The proposal uses public task as the lawful basis for core casework. It does not map each shared data field to a statutory function or explain why review staff need routine access to the complete originating record.

CommonCase will retain a linked person record while either body has an active case. Closed case material will follow the longer of the two existing schedules. This can extend retention by up to nine years for some Civic Support Agency records. The proposal has no process for a person to correct a false identity match across both bodies or restrict disputed data while a correction is investigated.

The Service Owner argues that a narrow transfer model will preserve the current burden. The Information Rights lead proposes purpose-specific evidence packages instead of full-record access. That option reduces data exposure but requires more workflow design and can delay urgent reviews.

## Security and resilience

CommonCase becomes a common dependency for two national services. It will connect to 68 internal and external interfaces. Twelve interfaces have no current owner. Seven use protocols that the architecture team plans to retire.

The design uses one identity tenant and one privileged support function. Logical controls separate the two bodies. A compromise of the support function could expose both case sets. The proposal includes immutable audit logs and quarterly access review, but no tested isolation procedure for operating one body while the other is under attack.

The target recovery time is 24 hours. Both bodies state that serious harm can occur after 8 hours for urgent cases. Paper fallback exists for new applications but not for reviews, evidence transfer, or payment changes.

## User and staff evidence

Research with 76 service users supports reduced repetition and faster hand-offs. Participants expected staff in one body to see information already supplied to the other. They also expected explicit notice and control over unrelated sensitive details. Assisted-digital users were underrepresented.

Staff support one search and fewer transfers. Review staff want full case access. Front-line staff report that broad access could discourage people from disclosing sensitive information needed for support. Accessibility testing found that the prototype improved keyboard navigation but made case history difficult to understand on a small screen.

## Open matters for assessment

The accountable officer asks the Board to assess whether the proposal should proceed to the departmental board, what conditions must precede approval, which risks need accountable acceptance, and where dissent between affordability, delivery confidence, information rights, security, legal authority, and user outcomes must be preserved.
